eulogy to sprinkles cupcakes

I feel bad for this stupid little cucumber on my birthday card, staring at me, so happy for me. "You're as cool as a cucumber! Have a happy birthday!" Oh, cucumber, thank you! I think you're cool too! Unfortunately, in a week, I won't feel anything when I read your message. My aunt and uncle didn't buy this card so you could talk to me for them. Their note is right there, blatantly overshadowing yours. Cucumber, I love you today, but tomorrow you're getting tossed in the Drawer and forgotten, along with all the other mascots of birthdays past.

At least he's not alone. Maybe they're all having a delightful little party in that drawer. I won't check. After what I did, why would I be invited?

Last semester, after an exam that could have gone better (which, admittedly, is most of them), I figured I'd treat myself to a cupcake courtesy of the on-campus Sprinkles Cupcakes ATM! Which is just a fancy way of saying “cupcake vending machine.” And, like any vending machine, it's out of order a lot, but it always comes through when I need it to. But that day, I saw the paper sign before I could even read it, so I knew I wasn't getting a cupcake. That’s okay, they’ll fix it next week. Just kidding, no they won’t, the entire company went out of business and it’s closed forever. Oops!

It's fine, there are other places to spend $5.75. But I wasn't as upset as I wanted to be. Which was upsetting.

I found my first love at Sprinkles Cupcakes, at a strip mall in Fremont, California. We’d go all the time, I think. I don’t know. At the time, I had no real sense of the world around me. That was my parents’ job. So, of course, the details escape me, but I was probably like 6 or 7, and she... well, she was red velvet. I still remember how she looked. She had white cream frosting, and she was topped off with a single sprinkle! Concentric circles, red and light blue. That was their thing, Sprinkles. Circles. Kind of a minimalist, Hallmark card aesthetic. And their logo screamed girl's school planner. Now, let’s move on from this analogy before I inevitably end up talking about eating women.

Of course, every time I got a cupcake at that ATM, I got red velvet. And every time, I felt like I should have felt like a kid again. But the cupcake never tasted like childhood as much as it did cupcake. Maybe it was just too long ago. No, that's not right. How can I not be the person I was 15 years ago? It's still me. I'm that kid, even if I don’t really remember him. Don't Ship of Theseus me here.

I'll let you in on a secret: I don't think goodbyes are ever proper. When do you really leave something behind forever? Don't tell me you've never been convinced by something or someone, maybe even a particularly heartfelt tweet, that you are the amalgamation of everything you once were and everyone you've ever met. But, no matter how hard you try, you can’t really be everything. Some things have to go, right?

So what do you say to the people and the things you can't remember? Do you apologize for renting out their permanent residence to something or someone more important? Do you say goodbye? Or do you fight tooth and nail to keep them there?

I didn't get to say goodbye to Sprinkles. Not when they went out of business, and not when they stopped meaning something to me. I guess things don't really tell you when they do that. It happens silently. Behind your eyes, where you can never see, and they never leave a note.

So it's nice when things get to move back in, like... oh! Well, that takes care of the callback. (Of course, it’s two Hallmark card mascots.)

I used to see Hoops and Yoyo every time I walked through the card aisle at Target, and that was that. If I ever had a card of theirs, it's in the Drawer. I'm sorry, I didn't know we'd be such good friends... but I'll spare you the missed connection. Oddly, there was something so familiar about their e-cards and their videos. It's like I was 6 again, looking at that stuff, wondering why those two made me so happy.

Hoops and Yoyo graced their last Hallmark card in 2015. But today, they live on in the DNA of Guava, the little pink bunny that runs this website! I know it's silly, but it's important to me. This is me not wanting to forget them. Not wanting to forget the part of me that liked them when I was 6. I will make sure they are still part of the amalgamation that is me. Through me, anything can live on... even the versions of me I left behind.

Maybe through this, Sprinkles Cupcakes will live on. Which means I was wrong. It does mean something to me. And so, even if I'll never eat another red velvet cupcake of theirs again, I won't say goodbye. Because why say goodbye to something that never truly left in the first place?

You were always invited to that party in the drawer. You just stopped wanting to show up.
